# GEB cover-letter points (5 bullets)

- Continental-scale test across 116 North American breeding-bird species
  (BBS, multi-decade route-level panel) of what lagged-state predictors in
  species distribution models actually measure.
- Explicit decomposition of lagged-state predictability into persistent
  route-level spatial heterogeneity vs genuine temporal history, using a
  2×2 design (leave-one-year-out vs forward-chaining × with/without route
  demeaning) — most apparent "history" is spatial persistence.
- Prospective validation under a strictly leakage-free forward-chaining
  design (train-only preprocessing, common evaluation samples) bounds the
  genuine lag-1 signal at ≈ +0.006 median History Gain.
- A strong biological contrast: route-level occupancy shows no hysteresis
  (matched-environment contrast = 0 in all definable species), while
  abundance exhibits robust state dependence (+0.63 log-count, 40/41
  species CI > 0) — distribution persistence and population dynamics are
  different phenomena.
- Direct implications for how lagged-state terms in SDMs and
  climate-redistribution forecasts should (and should not) be interpreted
  — relevant to GEB readers working on range dynamics, macroecological
  forecasting, and monitoring-data inference.
