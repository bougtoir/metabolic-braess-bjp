# Reviewer-perspective critical review — revised KOTHA manuscript (post-simulation)

Review date: 2026-08-28
Manuscript: "The KOTHA Framework: A Simulation-Based Approach to Quantifying and Correcting Structural Information Loss in Randomized Controlled Trial Meta-Analyses"
Source files: `build_paper_cct.py`, `build_paper_clinical_trials.py`, `validation/simulation_study.py`, `validation/run_validation.py`

---

## Executive summary

The revised manuscript adds (i) a transparent formal causal model of structural information loss and (ii) an operating-characteristics simulation that compares KOTHA's power-prior combination rule against RCT-enrolled-only, observational-only, and the target (all-RCT) estimators. The simulation is fully wired into the reproducible pipeline and all numbers flow from code. The revised title keeps "KOTHA" and now foregrounds the simulation-based, methodological value-add. The narrative is now much stronger on novelty: it quantifies, rather than only illustrates, why standard RCT-meta-analysis can fail when enrollment selects lower-risk populations.

Remaining risks are mostly journal-fit and framing issues, not fabrication or irreproducibility.

## 6. Journal fit and next-target recommendation

The manuscript has now been desk-rejected by six journals in the clinical-epidemiology / methods-for-trials space:

- Journal of Clinical Epidemiology
- Research Synthesis Methods
- BMC Medical Research Methodology
- Contemporary Clinical Trials
- Contemporary Clinical Trials Communications
- Clinical Trials

Because the empirical cases are retrospective, the clinical-impact hook is weak for clinical-trialist journals. The simulation strengthens the methodological contribution, so the next best targets are **general-methods / open-access journals that value reproducible computational methods** rather than trial-design application journals.

### Recommended next journals (in order)

1. **Trials** (BMC / Springer Nature) — Methodology article type
   - Presents new computational/methodological approaches in trial conduct, design, or analysis.
   - Abstract max 350 words, structured Background/Methods/Results/Conclusions/Trial registration.
   - Requires a "Declarations" section with ethics, consent, availability, competing interests, funding, authors, acknowledgements.
   - No strict figure/table limit; figures as separate files.
   - Open access; broad methods readership.
   - Fit: KOTHA is explicitly a trial-design methodology; the simulation is a reproducible computational advance.

2. **BMC Medical Research Methodology** (revised resubmission not encouraged after rejection, but if the editor desk-rejected because the contribution seemed incremental, the new simulation may be enough for a *de novo* submission only if the journal permits; otherwise skip.)

3. **PLOS ONE**
   - Accepts methods papers; strict reproducibility and data-availability requirements; lower desk-rejection barrier.
   - Fit: transparent simulation, open data, reproducible pipeline.

4. **F1000Research**
   - Open post-publication peer review; good for tool/framework manuscripts.
   - Fit: KOTHA is a modular framework; rapid publication possible.

5. **Scientific Reports**
   - Broad scope; methods manuscripts accepted.

### Tactical recommendation

Given the six prior desk rejections, the strongest remaining specialised fit is **Trials** (Methodology article type). The title, abstract, and declarations already align with the BMC *Trials* template. The cover letter should frame KOTHA as a **method for designing and interpreting future trials**, not as a retrospective meta-analysis re-interpretation. The simulation is the primary novelty and should lead the abstract and cover letter.
