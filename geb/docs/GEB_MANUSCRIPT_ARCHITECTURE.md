# GEB manuscript architecture

Target: Global Ecology and Biogeography — Original Article.
Scope: a single North American Breeding Bird Survey (BBS) macroecology study
across 116 species. Internal exploratory phases (1–9) are development history
and are not framed in the manuscript.

All empirical values referenced below are generated outputs of the Phase-9
post-fix pipeline (`phase9/results/tables/`, `phase9/src/`); nothing is
hardcoded. If the pipeline is re-run, the numbers quoted here must be
regenerated, not edited by hand.

## Core scientific message

The paper distinguishes four things that are routinely conflated when lagged
species state is used as a predictor:

1. apparent history dependence in distributions,
2. static spatial persistence,
3. genuine prospective lag-1 information,
4. abundance-level temporal state dependence.

Headline empirical findings (post-fix pipeline):

- naive / non-demeaned lagged-state models show substantial apparent history
  dependence (LOYO, no demean: median HG_A ≈ 0.30);
- most of it collapses once persistent route-level heterogeneity is removed
  (LOYO demeaned ≈ 0.02; forward demeaned ≈ 0.006);
- the primary prospective signal, HG_D (forward-chaining + route demean),
  is small: median ≈ +0.0059, 77% of species > 0, 34% with CI > 0;
- route-level occupancy hysteresis: unsupported (matched-environment
  occupancy contrast = 0.000 for all 41 definable species);
- regional aggregation does not rescue spatial memory (HG ≈ −0.04);
- delayed redistribution after climate anomalies: unsupported
  (env-only redistribution ≈ 0.31, history-aware ≈ 0.26);
- abundance retains strong temporal state dependence:
  median prior-state effect ≈ +0.63 log-count, CI > 0 in 40/41 definable
  species.

Intended ecological conclusion:

> Species distribution persistence and population abundance state dependence
> are not the same phenomenon. Apparent spatial memory in broad-scale
> distributions can be largely generated by persistent spatial heterogeneity,
> whereas abundance retains strong temporal state dependence.

## Introduction architecture (4 paragraphs + hypotheses)

1. Species distributions are commonly modelled as functions of contemporary
   environment; forecasts of climate-driven redistribution inherit this
   structure.
2. Adding lagged occurrence or abundance often improves prediction and is
   variously interpreted as inertia, memory, site fidelity, hysteresis, or
   delayed environmental tracking.
3. However, lagged state can simply encode persistent spatial heterogeneity:
   a consistently suitable route makes X_{t-1} predict X_t without any
   path dependence. Unless static structure is removed, the two are
   observationally confounded.
4. We use long-term BBS data across 116 species to decompose lagged-state
   predictability into static route persistence, prospective history signal,
   lagged environment, and occupancy- vs abundance-level state dependence,
   under both retrospective (LOYO) and prospective (forward-chaining)
   evaluation. End with hypotheses H1–H4.

## Primary hypotheses (stated before Results)

- H1: lagged previous state appears strongly predictive when persistent
  route-level spatial structure is not removed.
- H2: after controlling route-level persistence, prospective History Gain
  shrinks substantially.
- H3: if genuine spatial history dependence exists, lag-1 state still
  materially improves forward prediction after route demeaning, lagged-env
  control, common evaluation samples, and train-only preprocessing.
- H4: occupancy and abundance state dependence may differ; occupancy
  hysteresis may disappear under spatial control while abundance retains
  state dependence through demographic continuity.

## Results hierarchy (ecological logic, not analysis chronology)

- Result 1 — Apparent history dependence before spatial control.
  Distribution of naive HG (HG_A, HG_C): the phenomenon a reader might call
  "memory".
- Result 2 — Static spatial persistence explains most of it.
  2×2 decomposition (LOYO/forward × demean/none). Headline: LOYO
  0.30→0.02, forward 0.32→0.006, CV effect (C−A) ≈ +0.01 — static
  persistence, not future-information leakage, is the driver. Central figure.
- Result 3 — Prospective lag-1 information is small.
  HG_D distribution on 116 species; median ≈ +0.006, P>0 0.77, CI>0 0.34.
  Reported without exaggeration.
- Result 4 — Lagged environment does not restore a large history signal.
  HG after lagged-env control (M6−M5 ≈ +0.006), minimum-history 5/10/15-yr
  screens, expanding/rolling windows — all consistent.
- Result 5 — Occupancy hysteresis is unsupported.
  Matched-environment occupancy contrast = 0.000 in all 41 definable
  species. Key negative evidence.
- Result 6 — Abundance retains strong state dependence.
  Second major positive result: median +0.63 log-count, 40/41 definable
  species CI > 0. Framed as demographic state dependence, not spatial memory.
- Result 7 — Regional aggregation does not rescue spatial memory.
  Corrected regional HG ≈ −0.04. Pre-fix values appear only in a
  supplementary reproducibility note.
- Result 8 — Redistribution / hysteresis claims unsupported.
  env-only predicted redistribution ≈ 0.31 vs observed; history-aware
  ≈ 0.26; no convincing delayed redistribution or route-level hysteresis.
  Kept brief — supportive falsification, not the headline.

## Methods architecture

1. Data source — North American Breeding Bird Survey, years/routes used.
2. Species inclusion — final 116 species; explicit low-prevalence exclusions
   (nz_frac < 0.30 on surveyed route-years; 8 exclusions listed).
3. Response construction — surveyed present / surveyed absent /
   not-surveyed are three distinct states; unsurveyed cells are never
   coded as absences.
4. Environmental covariates — final variable list only.
5. History Gain — exact formula HG = OOS(M3) − OOS(M1).
6. Cross-validation — LOYO vs forward-chaining; train-only preprocessing
   (route-demeaning means and predictor column centring estimated from
   train only; complete-case evaluation — rows with missing response or
   covariates are dropped, there is no imputation).
7. Static spatial control — route demeaning / route means.
8. Common evaluation samples — M1/M3 evaluated on identical observations
   (asserted per species).
9. Lagged environment — current + previous-year environment.
10. Matched-environment analysis — occupancy and abundance separately,
    restricted to surveyed observations.
11. Simulation falsification — static persistence / true history /
    observation persistence scenarios.
12. Robustness — training length, windows, observers.

## Methodological wording rules

- Not "previous analyses were wrong" → "lagged-state predictability can
  arise from persistent spatial structure unless explicitly separated."
- Not "memory is false" → "apparent history dependence was largely
  attributable to persistent route-level heterogeneity."
- Not "abundance remembers" → "abundance exhibited strong temporal state
  dependence."

## Discussion architecture (7 paragraphs)

1. Main finding — lagged spatial state appeared strongly predictive; most
   apparent history dependence disappeared after controlling persistent
   route-level structure.
2. Why it matters — lagged occurrence is often interpreted biologically,
   but may proxy stable site quality; implications for SDMs, distribution
   forecasting, climate response, occupancy dynamics.
3. Occupancy vs abundance distinction — major conceptual contribution;
   occupancy path dependence unsupported, abundance state dependence
   strong. Abundance obeys demographic continuity
   (N_t = N_{t-1} × births − deaths + immigration − emigration); abundance
   state dependence need not imply spatial memory.
4. Implications for ecological forecasting — lagged-state predictors
   improve retrospective fit but should not be read as memory, hysteresis,
   or delayed climate tracking until static heterogeneity is separated.
5. Negative results matter — no occupancy hysteresis, no regional memory,
   no delayed redistribution; the falsifications sharpen interpretation.
6. Limitations — annual resolution, route-level (not individual) data,
   incomplete covariates, abundance observation process, BBS
   geography/taxa.
7. Future work — repeated population surveys + individual telemetry +
   finer temporal resolution to separate spatial memory, demographic
   inertia, habitat persistence. Brief.

## GEB broad-relevance hooks

Species distributions through time; interpretation of range persistence;
macroecological forecasting; climate-driven redistribution models;
spatially structured monitoring data. The paper is a biogeography paper
with a methodological spine, not a methods paper.

## What the manuscript must not rescue

No positive claims for ecological memory, hysteresis, delayed
redistribution, or regional persistence. The falsification is explicit;
the paper's strength is the three-way separation of apparent spatial
history dependence, persistent spatial heterogeneity, and abundance-level
temporal state dependence.
