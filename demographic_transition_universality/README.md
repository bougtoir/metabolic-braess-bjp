# Two pathways, many clocks: within-country heterogeneity and the limits of demographic-transition universality

A reproducible re-analysis accompanying a Perspective submitted to *Evolutionary Human Sciences*,
responding to Itao (2026), "Two universal pathways in demographic transition"
(https://doi.org/10.1017/ehs.2026.10054).

## Question
Itao (2026) reports that national demographic transitions follow two universal pathways — a
conserved product λ·e₀ before a mid-twentieth-century change point and a conserved
λ·exp(e₀/17) afterwards (λ = crude birth rate, e₀ = life expectancy at birth). We ask whether
this cross-national regularity is a placeless law or a by-product of aligning asynchronous
national transitions on a common calendar, and whether it survives (a) event-time alignment and
(b) descent to the sub-national level.

## Main findings (all regenerated by the code here)
1. Country fertility-transition onsets span ~185 years (IQR ≈ 58 y); only ~6% of countries begin
   within a decade of 1950. The sharp pooled "1950s" change point is an aggregation artefact.
2. Aligning each country at its own onset collapses the between-country dispersion of λ ~3.4-fold.
3. Under event alignment only the first conserved quantity (λ·e₀) tightens; the second does not —
   the two pathways are asymmetric.
4. Within single countries, regions at nearly identical e₀ differ in λ as much as the whole
   cross-national spread — the "law" fails within countries.

## Reproduce
```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
bash run_all.sh
```
Outputs land in `results/` (JSON/CSV), `figs/` (PNG/SVG + `figures_EN.pptx`) and `manuscript/`
(the `.docx`). No numeric result is hard-coded; every number in the manuscript is read from
`results/`.

## Pipeline
| script | purpose | key output |
|---|---|---|
| `scripts/common.py` | shared data loading / constants | — |
| `scripts/s02_reproduce_baseline.py` | pooled calendar segmentation (paper baseline) | `results/baseline_calendar.json` |
| `scripts/s03_transition_onset.py` | country-specific transition onsets | `results/country_onset.csv` |
| `scripts/s04_event_alignment.py` | calendar vs event-time collapse & conserved-quantity CV | `results/event_alignment.json` |
| `scripts/s05_changepoint_uncertainty.py` | bootstrap/posterior of the single change year | `results/changepoint_uncertainty.json` |

| `scripts/s07_figures.py` | figures | `figs/*.png|svg` |

| `scripts/s09_manuscript.py` | EHS Perspective manuscript | `manuscript/*.docx` |

## Data sources (all public)
- **Gapminder** national crude birth rate and life expectancy — https://www.gapminder.org/data/
  (bundled under `data/`).
- **Eurostat** regional database (NUTS2) — life expectancy `demo_r_mlifexp`, crude birth rate
  `demo_r_gind3` — fetched live and cached under `data/eurostat/`.
- **US** state life expectancy (NCHS/CDC, https://data.cdc.gov) and crude birth rate (US Census
  Bureau Population Estimates Program). US crude birth rate needs a free `CENSUS_API_KEY`.
- **Human Mortality Database** sub-population series (East/West Germany; NZ Māori/non-Māori) —
  https://www.mortality.org. Raw HMD micro-files are **not** redistributed here; the derived
  annual indicators are written to `results/subnational_hmd.csv`. Set `HMD_ROOT` to a local HMD
  copy to regenerate them.

## Notes
- `data/` contains the bundled Gapminder CSVs used by the paper; sub-national inputs are fetched
  at run time and cached.
- The onset estimator is a descriptive device; `s10_sensitivity.py` shows the conclusions are
  robust to its tuning.
