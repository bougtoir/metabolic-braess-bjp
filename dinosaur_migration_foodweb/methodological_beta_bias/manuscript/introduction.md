# Introduction

Beta diversity — the compositional turnover of communities across
space — is increasingly used to infer spatial ecological structure in
fossil assemblages: habitat breadth, niche partitioning, provinciality
and even organismal mobility are now routinely read off
community-similarity gradients built from presence–absence matrices.

Unlike modern ecological datasets, however, fossil assemblages differ
strongly and systematically between the groups being compared. Guilds
differ in regional gamma diversity, in dominance structure, in
achievable taxonomic resolution, in the duration of time their
collections average over, and in preservation and sampling structure.
Each of these properties is set by the fossil record's architecture
before any biology is read from it.

These properties can mimic or erase ecological turnover differences.
Yet although each bias is individually recognised — uneven richness,
a dominant taxon, coarse taxonomy, time averaging — the magnitude of
their joint effect on *guild-level contrasts* has rarely been
quantified, and no standard diagnostic sequence exists for separating
structural from ecological contributions before interpretation.

Here we quantify that distortion directly. As a worked example we use
the Upper Jurassic Morrison Formation, where an initially strong
guild-level beta-diversity contrast between predatory and herbivorous
dinosaurs (Δβ = −0.550) motivated the deeper decomposition presented
here — but the Morrison result is the case study, not the claim.

Our objectives are to (i) quantify, under controlled simulations with
known ground truth, how strongly gamma-diversity imbalance, dominant
taxa, taxonomic lumping, temporal averaging and asymmetric sampling
distort guild-level beta-diversity contrasts; (ii) identify the
conditions producing false differences, sign reversal and signal
attenuation; (iii) decompose the Morrison signal empirically to
determine how much of it survives structural correction; and (iv)
propose a practical diagnostic workflow for fossil beta-diversity
inference.
