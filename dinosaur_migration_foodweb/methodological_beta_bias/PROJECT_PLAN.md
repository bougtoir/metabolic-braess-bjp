# Methodological beta-bias project

**Type:** methodological palaeoecology study (new primary route).
Not a rescue of the Allosaurus mobility hypothesis; the Nature
confirmatory chain stays closed (`../PROJECT_OUTCOME_ORIGINAL.md`,
`../NATURE_GO_NO_GO.md`).

## Research question

How strongly can taxonomic richness, dominant taxa, taxonomic resolution,
temporal averaging and sampling structure distort guild-level
beta-diversity contrasts in fossil assemblages?

The Morrison result is the worked example: an apparent
predator–herbivore spatial-structure difference (Δβ = −0.55) largely
disappears under sequential structural correction.

## Conceptual framework

    Δβ_obs = Δβ_eco + B_γ + B_D + B_T + B_L + B_S + ε

- B_γ: gamma-diversity (taxon-pool) bias
- B_D: dominant-taxon bias
- B_T: taxonomic-resolution bias
- B_L: temporal-duration / time-averaging bias
- B_S: sampling / preservation bias

Additivity is NOT assumed; terms are identified sequentially and by
factorial simulation.

## Working title

*Taxonomic and sampling structure can mimic spatial ecological signals in
dinosaur fossil assemblages*

## Provenance (kept prominent — it is the methodological contribution)

1. Original hypothesis: herbivore continuity > predator continuity
   (Δβ > 0), preregistered test design.
2. Observed Morrison: opposite (Δβ ≈ −0.55).
3. Exploration: Allosaurus-specific.
4. Independent validation: failed in Nemegt (Δβ_full = +0.094).
5. Mechanistic decomposition: raw Allosaurus exceptionalism largely
   disappears after structural adjustment.
